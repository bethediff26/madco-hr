# Corporate Expense Reimbursement Policy

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026  
**Last Revised:** July 15, 2026  
**Version:** 3.3  
**Approved By:** Chief Financial Officer / VP of Human Resources  
**Department:** Finance — Corporate Services; Human Resources  

---

## 1. Purpose

The Mad Company Corporate Expense Reimbursement Policy establishes the standards, limits, approval workflows, and submission requirements for business-related expenditures incurred by employees in the course of company business. This policy ensures responsible stewardship of corporate funds while enabling employees to conduct necessary business activities with timely reimbursement. All employees, contractors, and temporary staff who incur expenses on behalf of Mad Company must comply with the provisions herein.

---

## 2. Policy Scope

This policy governs all reimbursable business expenses including but not limited to: travel, meals, lodging, ground transportation, client entertainment, professional development, home office supplies, and technology subscriptions authorized for business use. Non-business expenses, personal expenditures, and items explicitly excluded under Section 6 are not reimbursable.

---

## 3. Home Office Stipend for Remote Workers

### 3.1 Annual Allocation

Remote workers who qualify under the Remote Work Policy receive a **$500 annual home office stipend**, disbursed in two equal installments of $250 each (June and December). This amount is in addition to any monthly internet reimbursement of up to $75 per month as detailed in that policy.

### 3.2 Permitted Purchases

Eligible home office purchases include ergonomic furniture components, external monitors or docking stations, noise-dampening equipment, desk lamps, cables and adapters, and portable workstations. Purchases must be reasonable and proportionate to the employee's role requirements.

### 3.3 Documentation Requirements

Employees must submit itemized receipts totaling at least eighty percent (80%) of each installment within the calendar quarter following payment. Receipts that do not meet the documentation threshold will result in prorated reduction of the disbursement or clawback of funds already issued.

## 4. Business Travel Expense Guidelines

### 4.1 Meal Allowances and Per Diem Rates

Employees traveling on company business for meals are reimbursed according to the following per diem rates, aligned with the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) locality schedules:

| Category | Daily Per Diem Rate |
|---|---|
| Standard Domestic GSA Locality | Up to $75.00/day |
| High-Cost Metropolitan Area | Up to $95.00/day |
| International Travel (Foreign Per Diem) | Per GSA International Rate for the specific city/country |

Meals are reimbursed on a per diem basis, meaning no itemized restaurant receipts are required unless the expense exceeds the daily cap. Employees who consume meals provided by the employer or a third party (e.g., conference-hosted catering) must deduct the value of those meals from their daily per diem claim.

### 4.2 Lodging Limits

Lodging reimbursement during business travel is subject to the following maximum nightly rates:

| Category | Maximum Nightly Rate |
|---|---|
| Standard Domestic | $250.00/night |
| High-Cost City (per CBSX validated data) | $350.00/night |
| Conference or Event Hotel (when negotiated rate is available) | Actual cost up to the negotiated rate |

High-cost city determinations are based on Concur Benchmarking Solutions (CBSX) data, updated quarterly. Employees traveling to a high-cost locality may submit an expense report with justification for costs exceeding $250/night if CBSX confirms a rate above that threshold. If a hotel rate exceeds the applicable cap and no alternate lodging within budget is available in reasonable proximity, prior manager approval must be obtained via Workday before booking.

### 4.3 Ground Transportation

- **Domestic:** Reimbursement of actual costs for airfare (economy class), rail (coach), and rental car (economy or compact class). Rental cars require prior authorization; upgrade classes must be approved by the department head.
- **Mileage for Personal Vehicle Use:** $0.67 per mile (current IRS standard mileage rate) when a personal vehicle is used for business travel. Mileage logs with origin, destination, purpose, and odometer readings must accompany the expense report.
- **Airport Transfers:** Actual cost of rideshare or taxi services between airport and hotel/business destination, capped at $60 per one-way trip.

### 4.4 Client Entertainment

Client entertainment expenses are reimbursable when directly related to the active conduct of company business. Maximum per-person limits are $50 for informal meals and $100 for formal engagements (e.g., client dinners, conference receptions). All client entertainment claims require:
- Names and titles of all attendees.
- Business purpose documented in the expense report narrative.
- Receipts totaling the claimed amount.

## 5. Non-Reimbursable Items

The following categories are explicitly non-reimbursable under any circumstances unless expressly pre-approved by the CFO:

1. Alcohol beverages (including at client events; see Client Entertainment exception above for food-related exceptions with minimal alcohol cost).
2. Personal entertainment (movies, streaming subscriptions not shared for business use, video games, concert tickets).
3. Laundry and dry-cleaning expenses (unless travel extends beyond five consecutive days away from primary residence).
4. Spouse or companion companionship costs — no portion of lodging, meals, or transportation may be charged to the company when a spouse, domestic partner, family member, or other guest accompanies the employee on business travel unless that individual is traveling for bona fide business purposes and has been pre-approved in writing by HR and Finance.
5. Personal grooming and toiletries.
6. Health club memberships incurred during business travel (wellness stipends are covered under the Benefits Guide).
7. Fines, penalties, or traffic violations.
8. Expenses for items obtainable through other company programs (e.g., software purchases available through IT license agreements).

## 6. Expense Report Submission and Approval Workflow

### 6.1 Submission Deadline

All expense reports must be submitted through the company's expense management system (Concur) within **thirty (30) calendar days** of the date the expense was incurred. Late submissions will incur the following penalties:
- **31–60 days late:** Manager must provide written justification for the delay; reimbursement proceeds at the manager's discretion.
- **61–90 days late:** HR Finance reviews and approves only if exceptional circumstances (e.g., medical emergency, extended business travel) are documented.
- **Beyond 90 days:** Expenses are forfeited and not reimbursable under any circumstances.

### 6.2 Approval Thresholds

| Expense Amount | Approval Authority |
|---|---|
| Up to $250 (inclusive) | Direct manager |
| Over $250 up to $1,000 | Department head or director |
| Over $1,000 up to $5,000 | HR pre-approval + department VP |
| Over $5,000 | CFO approval required |

Any single expense item or consolidated report exceeding $250 requires manager review and digital authorization before reimbursement processing. Unusual expenses (e.g., non-standard conference registration fees, custom equipment purchases, foreign currency expenditures) require HR pre-approval regardless of dollar amount.

### 6.3 Expense Report Content Requirements

Each submitted Concur expense report must include:
- The business purpose for each line item.
- Dates of the associated activity or travel period.
- Names of all participants (for meals and entertainment).
- Itemized receipts for every expense exceeding $25, regardless of payment method.
- Credit card transaction details matching the receipt amount.

## 7. Per Diem Tracking and International Travel

### 7.1 Domestic Per Diem Tracking

Domestic meal per diems are automatically calculated in Concur based on the reported travel dates and GSA locality data. Employees need only confirm the location for each day; the system applies the correct rate without manual input.

### 7.2 International Per Diem

For international travel, Concur pulls from GSA's International Per Diem Rates database. Where GSA rates are not available (e.g., certain developing nations), the employee must submit actual receipts with a written justification for the claimed amount. International lodging and airfare claims require pre-trip authorization per the approval thresholds in Section 6.2.

### 7.3 Foreign Currency Conversion

Expenses incurred in foreign currencies are reimbursed at the exchange rate published by XE.com on the date of the transaction, or if unavailable, the first available rate in the seven-day window surrounding the transaction date. Exchange rate fluctuations below $1.00 per item are rounded; larger variances require a note explaining the discrepancy.

---

## 8. Policy Violations and Enforcement

Employees who submit expense reports containing false, misleading, or fabricated information may face disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment, in addition to potential clawback of all improperly reimbursed amounts and referral for internal audit review. Repeated violations (three or more instances within a rolling twelve-month period) trigger an automatic mandatory review by the Finance Compliance team and may result in suspension of expense privileges pending investigation.
