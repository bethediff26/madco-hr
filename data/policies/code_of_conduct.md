## 1. Purpose

The Mad Company Code of Conduct and Professional Standards Policy establishes the ethical expectations, behavioral standards, and professional conduct requirements for all employees, contractors, and representatives of the organization. This policy serves as the foundation of our corporate culture, defining what it means to uphold integrity, respect, accountability, and fairness in every interaction — whether in the office, remotely, at client sites, or on behalf of the company in public forums. Compliance with this policy is mandatory for continued employment and engagement with Mad Company.

---

## 2. Core Values and Ethical Principles

Mad Company operates according to five core values that inform every business decision and interpersonal interaction:

1. **Integrity:** We act honestly, transparently, and ethically in all dealings — with colleagues, clients, partners, and the public.
2. **Respect:** We treat every individual with dignity, regardless of role, background, identity, or opinion.
3. **Accountability:** We take ownership of our actions, admit mistakes, and follow through on commitments.
4. **Inclusion:** We actively foster an environment where diverse perspectives are valued and everyone can thrive.
5. **Excellence:** We hold ourselves to the highest professional standards in everything we do.

These values are not aspirational slogans; they are operational requirements that guide daily decision-making and performance evaluation.

## 3. Professional Conduct Standards

### 3.1 Workplace Behavior

All employees are expected to maintain a professional, productive, and respectful work environment. Prohibited behaviors include but are not limited to:
- Harassment, intimidation, bullying, or hostile conduct of any kind.
- Discriminatory remarks, jokes, gestures, or imagery based on protected characteristics (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, sexual orientation, gender identity, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law).
- Physical violence, threats of violence, or aggressive behavior.
- Unauthorized possession, use, or distribution of illegal substances on company premises or during company business.
- Excessive profanity, derogatory language, or offensive communication in any work-related channel (in-person, email, chat, video conference).

### 3.2 Dress Code and Appearance

Mad Company follows a business-casual dress code for all office-based activities. Employees are expected to present themselves in a clean, neat, and professional manner appropriate to their role and client-facing responsibilities. Client-facing roles require business-professional attire during meetings, presentations, and site visits. Remote employees must maintain camera-appropriate appearance during video conferences.

### 3.3 Punctuality and Attendance

Employees are expected to arrive at their scheduled start time and adhere to designated work hours. Consistent tardiness or unexcused absences constitute a performance issue subject to progressive discipline. Employees who anticipate being late must notify their manager as early as possible. Remote employees must be available on all scheduled communication channels during core working hours (10:00 AM – 3:00 PM local time).

### 3.4 Use of Company Property and Resources

Company resources — including equipment, software licenses, internet bandwidth, email systems, and intellectual property — are to be used primarily for business purposes. Limited personal use is permitted so long as it:
- Does not interfere with job performance or productivity.
- Does not violate any company policy or applicable law.
- Does not create security risks or consume excessive resources.
- Does not involve illegal, defamatory, or harassing content.

## 4. Anti-Harassment and Non-Discrimination Policy

### 4.1 Zero-Tolerance Commitment

Mad Company maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward harassment and discrimination of any form. This prohibition applies to all aspects of employment, including hiring, compensation, promotion, training, assignment, discipline, and termination. Harassment includes unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment or affects employment decisions.

### 4.2 Scope of Protection

This policy protects employees from harassment by:
- Supervisors or managers (including VP-level and C-suite executives).
- Co-workers at any level.
- Contractors, vendors, clients, or other non-employees with whom the employee interacts in the course of business.
- Third parties whose conduct affects the workplace environment.

### 4.3 Reporting Harassment

Employees who experience or witness harassment should report the behavior through any of the channels described in Section 6. Reports may be made anonymously where permitted, though anonymous reports may limit the company's ability to conduct a thorough investigation. The company will promptly investigate all harassment complaints and take appropriate corrective action, which may range from counseling and training to suspension, demotion, or termination depending on the severity and frequency of the violation.

### 4.4 Retaliation Prohibition

Mad Company strictly prohibits retaliation against any individual who reports harassment in good faith, participates in an investigation, or opposes discriminatory practices. Retaliation is itself a serious policy violation subject to disciplinary action up to and including termination. Employees who believe they have been retaliated against should report the matter immediately through the channels in Section 6.

## 5. Conflicts of Interest

### 5.1 Personal Financial Interests

Employees must disclose any personal financial interest that could conflict with, or appear to conflict with, their duties to Mad Company. This includes:
- Ownership of more than five percent (5%) in any vendor, customer, or competitor.
- Immediate family employment with a competing organization or significant business partner.
- Outside board positions, consulting engagements, or secondary employment that competes with or diverts time from company responsibilities.

### 5.2 Gifts and Gratuities

Employees may not accept gifts, favors, or gratuities from vendors, clients, or contractors exceeding **$75 in value** per occasion. Employees who receive offers of gifts above this threshold must decline them politely and report the offer to their manager and the Compliance team within five (5) business days. Giving gifts to company employees is also capped at $75 per occasion; any offer exceeding this amount should be directed to the Compliance team for disposition.

### 5.3 Outside Employment

Employees may engage in outside employment or business activities only if:
- The activity does not compete with Mad Company's business.
- It does not use company time, equipment, resources, or confidential information.
- It does not create an actual or apparent conflict of interest.
- Prior written disclosure is submitted to and approved by the employee's manager and HR.

### 6.2 Ethics Hotline Administration

The Mad Company Ethics Hotline is administered by an independent third-party provider to ensure confidentiality and impartiality. Reports submitted through the hotline are reviewed weekly by the Compliance team and escalated to the General Counsel or VP of Human Resources as appropriate. Reporters may opt to receive follow-up communication regarding the status of their report if they provide a contact method during submission.

### 6.3 Good Faith Reporting Protection

Employees who report concerns in good faith — even if an investigation does not substantiate the allegation — are protected from retaliation and adverse employment action. Reports made with malicious intent or known to be false are subject to disciplinary action.

## 7. Confidentiality and Information Handling

### 7.1 Duty of Confidentiality

All employees have a continuing duty to protect Mad Company's confidential information, trade secrets, proprietary data, and client information both during and after employment. Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information — whether intentional or negligent — constitutes a serious policy violation.

### 7.2 Social Media and Public Communications

Employees must not disclose company confidential information on social media, personal blogs, or public forums. When identifying themselves as Mad Company employees in personal online profiles, employees should include a disclaimer that their views are their own and do not represent the company. Employees who serve as official company spokespeople must coordinate all public communications through the Corporate Communications team.

## 8. Compliance and Enforcement

### 8.1 Progressive Discipline Framework

Violations of this Code of Conduct are addressed through the following progressive discipline framework, though the company reserves the right to skip steps based on severity:

| Level | Action | Applicable For |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verbal counseling and documentation | First-time minor violations |
| 2 | Written warning in personnel file | Repeat minor violations or first moderate violation |
| 3 | Final written warning with performance improvement plan | Continued violations after Level 2 |
| 4 | Termination of employment | Severe violations, repeated Level 3 infractions, or gross misconduct |

### 8.2 Severe Violations

The following constitute severe violations warranting immediate termination consideration:
- Physical violence or threats in the workplace.
- Sexual harassment or assault.
- Theft, fraud, or embezzlement of company or colleague assets.
- Intentional data breach or unauthorized access to confidential systems.
- Discrimination or harassment based on protected characteristics that is severe or pervasive.
- Falsification of company records, time sheets, or expense reports.
- Violation of export control laws, sanctions, or anti-corruption statutes.

### 8.3 Policy Acknowledgment

All employees must acknowledge receipt and understanding of this Code of Conduct annually through the learning management system. New hires must sign the acknowledgment during their first week of employment. Failure to complete annual acknowledgment may result in suspension of system access until compliance is achieved.
